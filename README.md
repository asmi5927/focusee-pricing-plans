## Which FocuSee plan should you buy?

Take **Standard** if you record on one machine and never touch the auto captions or the AI cleanup. Screen captures of bugs, short clips for a changelog, the occasional demo for a client. $49.99 a year for unlimited 4K recording is a fair price on its own.

Take **Advanced** for everything else. Course lessons, YouTube tutorials, documentation walkthroughs, sales demos, anything where you would otherwise spend twenty minutes cutting out pauses and typing subtitles by hand. The 300 credits a month is enough for steady use without topping up, and three computers covers a desktop plus a laptop plus one spare. This is the plan the pricing table is built around, and it shows.

Take **Lifetime** only if all three of these are true: you will still be recording in three years, you don't lean on AI credits every month, and you hate the idea of a subscription more than you hate paying $199.99 up front. That's a real group, but it's smaller than the marketing implies.

Two groups should skip the paid plans entirely. If you record a couple of clips a month and just need something in a browser, iMobie runs a free FocuSee Online version with no registration, and it handles basic screen recording and export. ## What reviewers and long-term users complain about

On G2 the product sits at 4.6 out of 5, and the praise is consistent: the automatic zoom on mouse clicks removes a whole editing step, and recording camera, microphone and screen at the same time without sync work is the feature people mention first. An online educator quoted on the official site describes exactly that workflow, recording once and needing no second editing pass.

The criticism is consistent too, and it's mostly about pricing rather than the software. In the product's own subreddit, a thread titled "Enough With Subscriptions. Just Give Us the Tool" argues that $199 for a lifetime license is too much for something that should be a simple offline tool. iMobie replied in that thread, acknowledging that pricing has never been fixed and has been adjusted repeatedly based on feedback, and that features have piled up beyond what some users asked for.

Three things to weigh before paying:

The credit system is metered and not obvious. AI features draw from a pool, and on Standard the pool starts empty. If your workflow depends on auto captions for long recordings, estimate the usage before committing to a year.

The lifetime license is tied to a major version. Access covers 2.x, and major future versions cost extra. That turns "lifetime" into "as long as you're happy with what you bought," which is a defensible policy but rarely what buyers assume.

Renewal prices don't move down. If you came in on a first-year discount, the jump at renewal is real and roughly 50% higher than what you paid. Budget for the list price, not the promo price.

## FocuSee FAQ

**Is there a working FocuSee coupon code?**
Not one I could verify. Discounts come through iMobie's own first-year promotions, which apply to new Standard and Advanced yearly purchases and not to renewals or upgrades.

**How much is FocuSee per month?**
Paid yearly, Standard works out to $4.17 a month and Advanced to $6.67. A monthly billing option exists, but the annual plans are the cheaper way to run it.

**Can I install one license on several computers?**
Standard covers one, Advanced covers three, and the lifetime license covers five. Activation is per machine, on Windows or Mac.

**What happens when the AI credits run out?**
Credit-based features stop until the next monthly issue on Advanced, which releases 300 credits a month, or until you buy more. Lifetime licenses come with 1,000 credits total and don't refill.

**Does the lifetime license include future versions?**
No. It covers FocuSee 2.x. A major version release later would be a separate purchase.
